# Brand and Visual Direction

## Brand personality

Monierave is:

- Calm, not passive.
- Premium, not luxurious.
- Precise, not technical.
- Warm, not playful.
- Modern, not trend-dependent.
- Protective, not alarming.
- Confident, not loud.

The product should communicate that money is being handled deliberately.
Whitespace, alignment, restrained language, and stable status presentation do
more trust-building work than visual effects.

## Visual concept: The Quiet Ledger

The visual language takes cues from carefully ruled ledgers, security paper,
architectural wayfinding, and modern editorial systems:

- Fine rules organize data without enclosing every item in a card.
- Warm mineral backgrounds prevent the clinical feeling of pure gray.
- Deep evergreen anchors navigation and important actions.
- A bright but controlled jade accent indicates forward action.
- Editorial serif typography appears only where reflection and orientation are
  useful.
- Data-heavy areas remain sans-serif, aligned, and numerically stable.

This direction is distinctive without turning banking into a visual spectacle.

## Logo and wordmark direction

Phase 2 does not create a final logo. The recommended direction is a restrained
wordmark with one custom detail inspired by a ledger tick, secure enclosure, or
balanced pair. Avoid:

- Currency symbols as the primary mark.
- Coins, bank buildings, shields with checkmarks, and upward charts.
- Gradients inside the core mark.
- Fine details that disappear below 24 pixels.

The product icon must work as a single-color mark before color variants are
considered.

## Photography and illustration

### Marketing

Use documentary-style images of real working, family, and small-business
contexts with natural light and restrained grading. Images support a financial
story rather than showing generic smiling users holding phones.

### Product

Prefer diagrammatic, paper-cut, or softly modeled geometric illustrations for
empty and educational states. Illustration should explain:

- What an account is.
- Why verification is required.
- What happens after a transfer.
- Why an account cannot be closed.

Do not use illustration when a clear sentence and action are sufficient.

### Security

Security imagery uses enclosure, layered boundaries, and controlled pathways.
Do not use threatening locks, hackers, warning tape, or surveillance imagery.

## Surface language

Pages are composed from open sections and ruled groups rather than a grid of
floating cards. Cards are reserved for objects with a meaningful boundary:

- An account summary.
- A beneficiary.
- A security session.
- A transfer review.

Tables, filter bars, headings, and navigation should not each be placed inside
separate rounded containers.

## Data-visualization direction

Charts are used only when they answer a real financial question more quickly
than a table. The initial dashboard does not add charts merely to fill space.

When charting becomes justified:

- Prefer direct-labelled lines and bars.
- Use consistent time direction and zero baselines where interpretation
  requires them.
- Use color plus line style, marker, pattern, or label.
- Provide exact values in an adjacent table or accessible summary.
- Keep grid lines quiet and typography consistent with transaction data.
- Avoid 3D charts, gauges, donut charts with many segments, decorative
  sparklines, and red/green-only comparisons.
- Never imply investment growth, forecasting, or spend categories unsupported
  by backend data.

## Visual hierarchy

The hierarchy is intentionally shallow:

1. Page purpose and primary financial fact.
2. Current status and next safe action.
3. Supporting data and navigation.
4. Tertiary explanation and metadata.

Only one filled primary action appears in a focused task region. Secondary
actions use outlines or text treatment. Destructive actions never borrow the
primary jade color.

## Experience-specific direction

### Marketing pages

- Editorial serif headlines.
- Asymmetric grids with strong baseline alignment.
- Deep evergreen bands used sparingly.
- One visual idea per section.
- Motion tied to storytelling, not scroll decoration.

### Authentication and verification

- Form remains the visual and keyboard priority.
- Desktop may use a 55/45 split with the form on the calmer side.
- Mobile removes decorative visual regions before reducing form clarity.
- Verification pages use a focused single-column layout.

### Banking dashboard

- Sans-serif typography only except rare welcome copy.
- Open page canvas with ruled data groups.
- Current posted balance is prominent but not billboard-sized.
- Account status stays adjacent to account identity.
- Navigation and data alignment remain stable between routes.

### Transactional forms

- One decision per section.
- Persistent review summary on wide screens.
- Clear conversion between entered major units and backend minor units.
- No decorative background motion.

### Security-sensitive confirmation

- Narrower content measure.
- Strong consequence heading.
- Explicit object identity and resulting state.
- Calm neutral background; danger color is limited to consequence and action.

### Mobile

- Content leads; branding recedes.
- Full-width sections replace nested cards.
- Bottom navigation remains stable outside focused confirmations.
- Sticky action regions account for safe-area insets and keyboard overlap.

## 3D boundary

The future transparent security-vault and virtual-card composition fits the
authentication art direction, but it is not a Phase 2 deliverable. Phase 2
approves only these constraints:

- Transparent background.
- Restrained evergreen and jade light accents.
- Frosted glass, brushed metal, and soft matte materials.
- No financial data rendered inside WebGL.
- Form usability and loading are independent of the scene.
- Static fallback is visually intentional, not an error placeholder.
